Reading the dial

The dial is an astrolabe, built like the astronomical clock in Prague (1410), and set for your place. The centre is the north celestial pole; south, where the Sun stands at noon, is at the top.

  • Blue is the sky above your horizon. Orange is twilight (AURORA at dawn, CREPUSCULUM at dusk). Black is night.
  • The golden hand carries the Sun and points to your time on the Roman ring: XII at the top is noon, at the bottom midnight.
  • The curved golden lines with 1 to 12 are the old unequal hours: the daylight split into twelve, longer in summer, shorter in winter.
  • The off-centre ring is the zodiac, the Sun's yearly path. It turns with the stars, once a sidereal day.
  • The Moon rides its own hand and shows its real phase. The small star marks sidereal (star) time.
  • The outer gothic ring 1 to 24 is Old Bohemian time: hour 24 is sunset, so it shifts through the year.
  • The gold band round the edge is your focus session filling up. The bell rings when it is full.
  • Coming up in the sky lists eclipses, meteor showers, planets meeting and the space station passing over you. When the station is up, it crosses the background sky as a moving light.

Behind it is the real sky above you right now: stars, constellations, the Milky Way, the planets and the Moon.
